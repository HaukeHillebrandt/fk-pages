Reading too many posts causes premature cognitive decline

I’ve got trouble retrieving

I can’t quite remember exactly where and when I noticed it the first time, which is ironic, but it must have been when I was merely around 33 years old and I also I do remember the event itself very vividly: I was watching a documentary that was a little bit meh but everyone was talking about it and it had such a high RT score when around halfway through the film there was a BIG OL’ reveal: the documentarian uncovered a tickle torture porn ring! But it was not the tickle torture porn ring that took me by surprise, but that I had seen the exact same film before and forgotten the entire set-up and everything leading up to the big reveal.

How-oh-how could I have forgotten that tickle torture porn ring? How was that possible? Do I fashion myself an infovore and a man of culture? Yes. And yes, I have seen that Werner Herzog movie where the guy gets eaten by the bear. True, maybe I did watch too many movies then and it all became a bit of a blur but it wasn’t that that many. Do I love drinking? Well, also yes, but not so much that my axons were demyelinating. Was I just an old baldy then? Well, I was but I was hiding it well and I did get MSP. And I was only a third of a century old; why-oh-why was this shit already starting at this age?

My undergraduate major in social and cognitive psychology (a discipline now mostly discredited), too, is a bit hazy, but this was partly the concerta and partly because my English was still developing and there’s a strong relationship between language and memory. I do distinctly remember an experiment from undergraduate psychology about the causes of childhood amnesia being due to the lack of language development where they showed young kids like some really memorable event and they found that only the kids who had the words to verbalize it could recall it at a later stage. Would it replicate? ‘I want to believe’.

But the other thing I remember from that course on memory is that the reason for why we ‘forget’ things as we age. It’s not that we actually forget things, it’s that we mostly have trouble recalling.

When you’re very young, you only know one of white male actor, say Matt Daemon, because all you’ve watched is Good Will Hunting. But then you watch too many movies and you know so many white male actors, and when you try to retrieve it there’s a certain probability mass on each of them. What was his name again, that actor, what’s his face, the guy who played Bond after Pierce Brosnan? You’re pretty sure it’s not Michael Cera, but there it could be so many others that roughly fit the bill.

It’s unsettling, but you learn to live with it. You start masking ‘Oh yeah long time no see, how’s it going ehem…  oh, yeah, I remember you, but you have so many meetings at EAG.’  It’s not early-onset Alzheimer’s, but it’s not not early onset Alzheimer’s either, in that it’s progressive and spreads to other areas of life outside of film trivia. Like, true, you’ve always been a ladies man and you don’t have to brag, but it wasn’t that many (indeed far too few), yet never had you thought you’d forget any of them, but then you did (it’s good that you keep a list, again, not because there were so many, but due to my issue with the retrieval).

So what to do about it? Maybe it’s all just too much. Too much gunk, gunking up your brain. You need to unsubscribe. Unsubscribe from everything. Clear out the cache. Do a reboot, make room for new stuff, but be more selective. Maybe it’s time to settle down. No more Feeld (not that it’s ever been easy there), no more South Park. Get back on the horse, get back on Hinge, find a wife, and only watch the highbrow stuff, the stuff that’s edifying, only the major HBO shows. Only what Tyler Cowen recommends, nothing Scaruffi rates less than 7.2 or maybe 6.9. But even that’s too much isn’t it? And besides it’s too dry and heavy. There’s too much content that’s for sure. Maybe I could use spaced repetition to drill and solidify the facts about friends and loved ones and my godchildren and then I could play GTA VI when it comes out.

Maybe go to Inkhaven, read the greats. One of the organizers read thousands of essays, and he seems pretty stable still. But on some level that seems insane to me. Everyone’s writing so much… who has the time and wherewithal to read all of that?

I mean does anyone even subvocalize anymore? Are you really savouring the fiction you read? Or are we all merely skimming at this stage. Just getting through it. Come on Proust, just get to the god damn point already. What's the bottom line? BLUF! Apparently, Marcel becomes a novelist.

Do you ‘member when you were young and it was so joyous to read novels? Took your sweet time reading all those novels, didn’t ya? Subvocalized. Yeah, I ‘member. But do you remember when you first tried to get into speed reading in college? They gave you all these readings and then there were even optional readings but it's just too much. You spend hours in the library and you can't possibly get all the reading done. But maybe you're a completionist. You start doing the math: 'well I read 14 pages this hour, and the syllabus has x, and there are y courses so, how do I get through all the reading? How would anyone? Even your literature professor says ‘Even if you read 100 books a year, that’s just at most a few thousands during your life time.’ Your college girlfriend says the professor gives her the creeps. Not in a harrassy way but one time they had a meeting his glasses cracked for no apparent reason. He was kind of weird that guy. Why was he reading all these books for? I guess he was teaching literature. You start thinking: maybe I'm a slow reader... is there a way to speed that up? You look into speed reading. They tell you not to subvocalise. They tell you subvocalisation is the devil. They tell you subvocalization is for the actually imbecilic. But it's just kind of skimming. But like... aren't thoughts words? How would you not subvocalise? Apparently, ‘language is primarily a tool for communication rather than thought’, there was a Nature paper that I meant to read. But is that true for wordcels? Is speed-reading all bs like hypnosis? I remember they did test them on some tv show and gave some speed readers long books and asked them questions and they answered them correctly. But maybe they were just using some really high-level heuristics? There are probably no good papers on that. I call bs. Like sure you can skim. You get better at skimming with age too, especially w/ stuff that is highly structured like scientific papers. But the entropy in good literature should be sufficiently high that makes skimming somewhat pointless. It's like listening to podcasts and audiobooks. Some nonfiction I can listen to at 3x the speed because it's just so nonfiction drivel that doesn't cause much surprisal at all. But then literature... Why would you speed read that at all? Just to get the cliff notes? Why not read 'save the cat' and learn about the basics of narrative. I've never read 'save the cat' but I've always wanted to. Maybe if I had a more abstract theory of how narrative works I could be more like von Neumann? Or at least like Lena Dunham… she said Jenny Koenner told her everything about b-story lines and stuff. Meow.

A related thought I had about this recently (suave segway): what's the deal w/ all these kids who are like in their early 20s having like this agenda to have the most sophisticated taste in art possible by just approaching it really systematically and just read what Tyler Cowen and Scaruffi recommend? That's kind of fucked up. I was talking to one of these kids once and it seems like they've listened to everything and seen all the important films, but like, it does seem a bit weird. Don't you have to watch some bad movies sometime or discover stuff through serendipity? Don’t you need live experience? One of them said their favourite FJM song is ‘Leaving LA’. As if. Isn’t art kinda subjective or something? Isn't the best music the music that you listen to during formative moments of your life, like when that older american woman living above you recommends the magnetic fields? God she was fiiiine. First time you saw her you couldn’t stop staring and things happened because you still had fantasies and could act on your desires. Now you’re on SSRIs and GLP-1s and you’re like bread on a stick. Even friggin’ TLP tells you to read the primary sources, but maybe: fuck that? Maybe I don’t want to be led by TLP anymore. Maybe that’s the lesson. I just need to get my fantasies and healthy desire back. It’s all noise. It’s all too much. What do all these dead white guys know anyway. Didn’t they believe that women had more teeth than men and we should set widows on fire and isn’t that suggestive evidence that are wrong in all the other important ways as well? And it’s all so heavy and dry. I say: don’t read the research digests either. Brevity comes from selection not compression.

Don’t read anything at all I guess is the lesson. There, I said it. Touch some grass maybe. Writing on the other hand. Oh yes, writing is thinking. The stuff of thought. Start creating, stop consuming. But then what should you write about? I need inspiration. I recently came across the wikipedia article on philosophical fiction. Tempting no? Maybe I am in the market for a new novel after all. Or maybe I should give Knausgaard another try, I guess he’s in my reference class, but then what does he know? Maybe more Rachel Cusk. Maybe read only women, but not performatively. Like read even more Cusk but don’t tell anyone. Anybody got any good reading recommendations? Let me know in the comments. ba dum tsssssss 🥁.
