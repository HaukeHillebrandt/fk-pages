Is everyone traumatized or well adjusted?

Today, I had a disagreement with someone on the question of whether most people are well-adjusted (his argument) and fine, or whether most people are somewhat traumatized and fucked up (my argument).

To paraphrase his position, and maybe steelman a bit:

We’ve overcorrected on the mental health front and we shouldn’t medicalize stuff too much, and sometimes therapy can be counterproductive. Everything is a mental health issue today.

I think that’s of course true on the current margin in the WEIRD populations (cf. Jonathan Haidt’s The Coddling of the American Mind book).

Also psychoanalysis (which was the context of the conversation) is maybe not good for everyone on the current margin, and one can be retraumatized by it. Indeed I think there was evidence that sometimes suppressing trauma is actually the best course of action (citation needed but I can see it).

I’m not sure if I did such a good job at steelmanning this, but essentially this was the point.

I can’t quite recall how exactly we got onto it and how the conversation evolved, but he said that most people are quite alright, and I said well in our socioeconomic strata especially, this is a biased sample (I wasn’t as eloquent and am adding stuff here for my case obviously) and people are obviously fairly functional, but that most people are probably a bit traumatized.

He called for evidence that people back in the day when things were not medicalized as much and mental health aware were necessarily much worse off.

My main counter was that if you zoom out a bit in historical terms, we’ve both had a more traumatizing time (‘The past is a developing country’) and it coincided with people becoming better people roughly (~ ‘The Better Angels of our Nature’ story, or the ‘On the possibility of ongoing moral catastrophe’ paper).

But is this causal really? We did agree on a crux, because we were talking about how we used to hit children much more and that faded out over time, and that there are perhaps natural experiments in terms of policy on hitting children in school... did that coincide with better adjusted adults? Would be really interesting to see. Identification would be fairly rough. I guess the way I see it, maybe the older teachers who used to hit children might have just kept doing it or used other forms to scare younger children. Enforcement was probably quite difficult.

But also like... they ain’t no RCTs for the effectiveness of parachutes.

I also cited “qualitative evidence” about David Lynch: a friend told me the interpretation of the whole weird Lynchian way is that he was trying to capture how weird the people in the 50s were. And I was like, yes old people are very often very weird and I reckon very traumatized. This is not accounting for cognitive decline. I’ve got a lot of sympathy for old people. I often say if what happened to older people routinely happened to any of your friends in this generation, that would be the most salient feature about them in your mind. Like just listen to them. In some sense we don’t want to hear about it because society censors suffering really hard.

Germany was also a natural experiment in a way. Lots of fucked up shit happened in the GDR. And it did show. Many people were pretty traumatized and weird as a result.

I think that also holds true in less extreme cases. And so I think everyone should do psychotherapy like they do in Argentina.
