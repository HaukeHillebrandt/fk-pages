On the desire to change

I bought a book once, well, it was an Audible actually, but still, it had the title “On the desire to change” or something. I used to have quite a spell with stupid self-help books, which culminated embarrassingly late with listening to all of Oliver Burkeman, who I think is like a way out for people like me who had been stuck in a self-help maelstrom somehow (like Deep Work and stuff... Deep Work is pretty much the only self-help thing that actually works. But then Deep Work is also exhausting. So no such thing as a free lunch or a quick win).

But anyway back to “On the desire to change”... I can’t remember actually listening to the Audible, but I think I did. I vaguely remember it was more woo than I expected, but almost nothing else.

I would really like to change sometimes. But I wonder if you ever can? Change is, like, so difficult. Like, we’re all so stuck in our ways. Like, I’m talking about your overall vibe, and what you do, like the kind of humor you have and how you interact with people. It would be nice to blow some fresh air into things, and really mix up your personality no?

Talking about no quick fixes: meditation does really help with it. Like, the one tangible result from meditation that I got (not that I’ve meditated that much, but I do pride myself on having _sat_ one 5 day silent retreat just fyi just in case you were curious about my meditation credentials) was that you become less reactive, or at least I have, like it allows me to shut up and listen and actually focus on what the other person says. Generally, actually meditation is almost the only way of behavioral self-modification if you think about it:

we’re all just automatons being kicked by random influences. If you don’t sit still and try to observe your thoughts (or write them down and think really hard about them or have some other sort of practice), then you’re subject to external stuff. Like, you become a drone and you’ll drone on about the stuff you read on your teleprompter. Don’t regurgitate. Don’t plagiarize or take on loan.

We tire easily of our personalities, for we spend too much time with ourselves. For instance, in North America, it’s easy to fall into the ‘all-American’ personality trap, there does seem to be a certain type that has a lot of gravitational pull and sucks all the people who don’t put up strong defences of trying to retain some individuality into their local minimum, the median, the mid, the ‘mode’, the central tendency of the day. It’s the same in every country but the US is more of a mixture of cultures and there are also so many Americans that the all-American somehow seems particularly salient as a type. Escaping such attractor states is hard and can only be done by concerted effort of going against the grain. Going against the grain is hard. As you get older, you will fall off the bandwagon (e.g. you’ll overuse the same joke formula, you will, in other words, become your dad, making dad jokes).

You gotta keep on your toes, guard yourself from bad mimetic influences, but also keep abreast of some pop culture, you don’t want to be out of touch. You also don’t want to get caught in some weird subculture, but also not be normcore. You don’t want to get stuck in some ironic loop either. It has happened to me. It’s hard to get out of a personality once you’re in it. But method actors can change their entire personality. So maybe there is still hope. Just pick a persona you deem more edifying than your current one, and stay in character until it’s no longer an act.

“I can change, if it helps you fall in love” is an LCD Soundsystem quote. For people who like that band, the kids these days seem to have even more emphasis on individuality and developing a personality. Inflationary use of Gen Z slang, exaggerated physical mimicry, invent your own singsong prosody, oscillating between effeminacy and masculinity, extreme crassness, and grandiloquence. In terms of countersignalling, self deprecation is out, ironic boasting is in. Would you like to relate authentically? Why yes I lowkey want to. I’m just an honest, unconflicted, creative, and original nature lover who faces hard truths with little interest in status, religion, or material goods.
