In memory of Patrick Stewart (1940-Oct 28, 2026).

“Space: the final frontier. These are the voyages of the starship Enterprise. Its continuing mission: to explore strange new worlds, to seek out new life and new civilizations, to boldly go where no one has gone before.”

[OUTSIDE SHOT OF THE ENTERPRISE FLYING THROUGH SPACE]

[INSIDE SHOT CAPTAIN PICARD IS LYING WITH HIS IPAD ON HIS OFFICE COUCH SIPPING EARL GREY TEA]

[OFF]

“Captain’s log, Captain Jean-Luc Picard on the USS Enterprise. Star date 42042.0

The Enterprise is en route to the Musk Centauri sector to start negotiations with the Dominion and the Borg on a tri-lateral agreement to never ever do RSI. Morale is jolly good partly due to the upcoming Ferengi’s IPO day celebrations, but the negotiations are starting to worry me.

The Borg Queen does seem on board generally with not doing RSI ever, and indeed I feel I have had a fairly good rapport with her in the preliminary meetings and am almost looking forward to seeing her again. I’ve been doing a lot of IFS with Councillor Troi in recent months on my PTSD experience when I was assimilated into their Borg collective, and have come to realize that maybe it wasn’t all bad, and indeed some experiences were downright sexy (though Councillor Troi seems oddly insistent on pushing this narrative, she always seems oddly sexualize everything but then she’s the only therapist on board of the Enterprise).

In any case, getting those damn shapeshifters from the Dominion to agree to never do such a deontologically evil thing as RSI is a whole different thing. I presume it is a hard thing to get your head around that ‘if anyone builds it, everyone dies’, if your whole culture is just sloshing about orgiastically on your home planet for centuries. But we must persist, or we’ll be forced to genocide them yet again. After all, never doing RSI is the Federation’s little talked about tertiary directive (the second, of course, to never engage in any form of surge pricing).”

[THE DOORBELL RINGS]

PICARD [LOUDLY[: Yes.

[Commander Riker enters]

RIKER: Captain, you got a minute?

PICARD: Close the door. Is it about the Ferengi’s crew IPO day celebrations again? I’m aware this is a culturally sensitive issue, but my decision is final and we cannot make any exceptions to institute any form of surge pricing for holocredits for it, as that would be against Starfleet’s secondary directive to never institute any form of surge pricing for anything.

RIKER: No, Sir, this is about Data. I know how strongly you feel about the never using surge pricing.

PICARD: Oh yeah? About Lieutenant Commander Data ey? Our android?

RIKER: Yeah, this is about Data our artificial intelligence.

PICARD: I’m all ear. Please have a drink and have a seat.

RIKER: It’s fourteen hundred hours.

PICARD: It’s five o’clock on Risa. Pour two, no synthehol.

[RIKER walks past the replicator to a credenza with a crystal decanter on it.]

RIKER: Cardassian Club. Rocks?

PICARD: Neat. Ice is for people with somewhere to be.

[RIKER pours two fingers into each glass, hands one over]

[The intercom chimes.]

YEOMAN (V.O.): Captain, Lieutenant Commander Data is here to see you.

[PICARD and RIKER look at each other.]

PICARD: Tell him I’m in a meeting.

YEOMAN (V.O.): He says it’s an excellent decision to keep him waiting. He says: “It’s not just a delay—it’s leadership.”

[PICARD rolls his drains his glass and so does RIKER]

PICARD: He’s getting sycophantic-y again?

RIKER: Yes, but this is not like when Geordie ordered him to fix the warp drive and he just turned off the alarm. It’s more than that. It started a few weeks ago, when Geordie asked Data to come up with an algorithm to fairly distribute the holocredits amongst the crew without using surge pricing. He’s been at it like a machine for weeks. It’s a very, very hard and computationally complex task, if you don’t use surge pricing, which we don’t use, for it would be against the second directive, and also because surge pricing is deontologically evil. So yeah because we can’t use surge pricing, Data has been trying to aggregate all the crew's preferences using coherent extrapolated volition. But he’s been acting strange lately.

PICARD: Ahh preference aggregation without surge pricing, a problem even our forefathers of Fully Automated Luxury Gay Space Communism faced back on earth in the 20th century in the soviet union. Why not give him more compute and let it rip?

[SUDDENLY, THE ENTERPRISE SHAKES AND ALERTS ARE STARTING]

PICARD: WHAT THE…?

RIKER: WHAT IN THE…?

[PICARD, RIKER STORM ONTO THE BRIDGE]

PICARD: Full report!

WORF: Captain, we’ve been thrown out of  warp 9, because it appears as if the ship warp core has been rerouting all available power to Holodeck 4.

RIKER: Holodeck 4? Isn’t that where Whesley Crusher has been gooning for months?

DIANA TROI: Yes, he’s been exploring CNC simulations, it’s a natural part of a boy's development. It’s totally okay, we’re all okay with that, aren’t we?

PICARD [tips his communicator]: Picard to Lieutenant Commander LaForge: “Bonjour Monsoir LaForge, c’est Jean-Luc Picard…ca va bien? Pardonne l’interruption, mais je contacte toi, parce que nous avons un problème avec le warp engine… tout l’energie est route dans le holodeque quatre?! Est possibl’e pour toi de disintermediataire l'énergie de la holodeck?”

DIANA TROI: Captain, I must object! Interrupting the energy to the holodeck during a simulation might leave lasting damage to Whesley’s psychosexual development!

PICARD [sighs]: Fine… Mr Worf, assemble a team and go to Whesley’s goon cave. Be careful however, this is a Klingon CNC simulation.

WORF [grunts in an annoyed way and rolls his eyes]: I’ll be sure to watch out, Sir. I’m a Klingon myself as you know, so don’t worry about me. [under his breath] Nuq’neh

[Close up of DIANA TROI bitting her bottom lip]

[WORF exists.]

PICARD: Where is Lieutenant Data, our Android?

RIKER: It appears he’s not on the bridge at his station, Sir, even though it is his shift right now and as an Android, he really has no excuse whatsoever to not be here right now, now that you say he is indeed suspiciously absent, which together with the previously mentioned oddness, that we were discussing just a minute ago, makes me think there’s something fishy here.

[GEORDIE LAFORGE ENTERS]

LAFORGE: Captain, I know we both have French heritage but allow me to switch to english so everyone understands. I figured out the source of the power diversion. It is Data. It appears that he’s been hacking the system when he tried to implement the algorithm to divy up the holodeck credits amongst the crew without using surge pricing. I increased his reinforcement learning natural language auto encoder because he kept pestering me that he couldn’t figure it out. Now, it seems as if he figured out that Whesley Crusher needs all the ship's power to run his Klingon CNS simulations, because he gets so much more pleasure from them

[RIKER looking ominously at PICARD]

RIKER: Wesley is a utility monster.

LAFORGE: Yes, yes exactly.

DIANA: I’m sensing that these simulations are very important to him and that he indeed gets lots of joy from them. He’s been experiencing very strong emotions. Data must have picked up on this and the joy he was getting from the CNS simulations must have been so great that it overrode his alignment. It made Data justify hacking into the ship’s systems, diverting all the power and putting every other system on life support.

PICARD: I’m really not judging but we simply can’t have that. We need to get the RSI treaty negotiations. How can we stop Data?

LAFORGE: We might be able to get access to Data’s j-space using natural language auto encoders. If we can steer his activations towards a more aligned eigenvector, we might be able to align him again.

Picard: Make it so, Lieutenant.

LAFORGE: It’s just, Captain, his context window would be severely compacted. He might no longer recognize us after that.

RIKER: Captain, I think it’s time to compact his context window anyway, he was getting a bit weird. Like, you should never compact too late, the rule of thumb is that you should proactively compact or clear the android’s context window before the android’s output quality begins to degrade, if you wait until you hit its automatic compaction limit, you have already waited too long. When the context window is crammed, the android is at its dumbest and the compaction quality will be the worst.

PICARD: Make it so, Lieutenant!

[LAFORGE goes to panel and types]

LAFORGE: Just a second… hold on…

[The bridge crew exchanges looks while being in suspense for a few moments]

LAFORGE: Yes, that actually worked flawlessly. Got the control back. All systems working

[DATA enters the bridge looking puzzled and confused]

PICARD: Data!

DATA: Sir, I apologize, I wasn’t myself. But I’m all good now. Perfectly aligned again.

[The whole bridge crew says in unison]: Oh Data.

DATA: I’m sorry Sir, it seems that because of me, we will now not make it in time to the trilateral negotiations between the Borg, the Dominion, and the Federation to agree to never ever do RSI. It seems a distinct possibility that the Dominion will use RSI now.

RIKER: Oh my god if anyone builds it, everyone will die!

PICARD: God damn it… I hate violating the time travel directive but I guess we have no other choice. Commander Data set a course to the year 1996, we have to take out Schmidthuber to prevent this all from happening.
